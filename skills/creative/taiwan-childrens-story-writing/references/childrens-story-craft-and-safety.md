## Key Takeaways

### Age Appropriateness

Always identify the target age before writing. Younger children need concrete situations, short cause-and-effect, emotional labeling, repetition, and fast reassurance. Older children can handle richer motives, but still need hope and clarity.

Recommended bands:

- 0–2: rhythm, naming, warmth, sensory cues.
- 3–5: repetition, simple problem/solution, clear emotions.
- 5–7: friendship/family/school, mild suspense, visible cause-effect.
- 7–9: more agency, fairness, responsibility, teamwork.
- 9–12: identity, belonging, ethical tradeoffs, nuanced but hopeful endings.

### Moral Meaning

Children's stories work best when the lesson emerges through character action. Avoid heavy lecturing. Use one clear emotional center: kindness, courage, honesty, patience, responsibility, empathy, or respect for nature/community.

Preferred pattern:

1. Character wants something.
2. A problem appears.
3. Character tries an imperfect solution.
4. Character notices another feeling or consequence.
5. Character chooses better.
6. Ending shows repair, warmth, or growth.

### Safety

Avoid graphic violence, gore, horror framing, adult innuendo, self-harm methods, cruelty to animals, unsafe dares, and risky imitation. Mild fear can work if brief, contained, and resolved. If danger appears, model safe help-seeking and reassurance.

### Read-Aloud Style

Use short sentences, active verbs, concrete images, rhythm, repetition, and playful sound words. Read the draft aloud and cut anything that feels like an essay.

### Humor

Prefer surprise, repetition, role reversal, silly sounds, and gentle misunderstandings. Avoid humiliation, body-shaming, accent jokes, disability jokes, poverty jokes, or stereotypes.

### Inclusion

Represent characters with agency and individuality. Do not make villainy or foolishness correlate with body type, disability, accent, class, gender, ethnicity, nationality, or culture. Use sensitivity review for specific lived experiences when needed.

### Taiwan Localization

Use Traditional Chinese and Taiwan Mandarin by default. Prefer terms such as 腳踏車、捷運、便當、國小、幼兒園、公車、便利商店、阿公、阿嬤. Use Taiwan settings naturally: 夜市、騎樓、菜市場、圖書館、公園、早餐店、廟口、河濱、捷運站.

### YouTube / Platform Notes

For children's video content, decide whether the upload is “Made for Kids.” Avoid manipulative engagement asks, fearbait thumbnails, dangerous challenges, adult jokes hidden in child content, or direct pressure on children to buy or click. Keep metadata honest and calm.

## Practical Final Checklist

- Target age is clear.
- Language is Traditional Chinese with Taiwan usage.
- Conflict is age-appropriate and resolvable.
- Moral is shown through action, not sermon.
- Child protagonist has agency.
- No graphic, frightening, discriminatory, or imitable-risk content.
- Humor is playful, not mean.
- Ending gives warmth, repair, reassurance, or wonder.
- For story videos, paragraphs map to visual scenes and leave room for narration pauses.
