# Taiwan Children's Story Writing

## Overview

This skill helps turn an idea into a child-friendly story in **Traditional Chinese with natural Taiwan usage**. It is designed for picture books, read-aloud scripts, story videos, bedtime stories, and short educational fables.

The goal is not just to make the story cute. A good children's story should be clear, warm, safe, rhythmic, emotionally meaningful, and fun to hear aloud. It should carry a gentle lesson through character choices and consequences, not through lecturing.

Default voice: **繁體中文、台灣用語、幽默活潑、節奏輕快、不拖泥帶水、適合朗讀**.

## When to Use

Use this skill when the user asks to:

- Write a new children's story.
- Rewrite or polish a draft for children.
- Adapt a classic tale into Taiwan Traditional Chinese.
- Turn a lesson or moral into a playful story.
- Create scripts for illustrated story videos or bedtime narration.
- Check whether a story is too scary, violent, preachy, slow, or adult.
- Make a story more 台灣口語、童書感、好朗讀、有趣.

Do not use this skill for:

- Adult horror, dark fantasy, satire, or YA themes unless the user explicitly asks for an older-child adaptation.
- Clinical or therapeutic advice disguised as a story.
- Culture-specific stories about real communities when expert/sensitivity review is needed and unavailable.
- Platform-policy legal determinations; provide practical safety guidance but do not claim legal certification.

## Intake Checklist

If the user gives enough context, proceed with assumptions and label them. Ask only when missing information materially changes the result.

Minimum fields:

- **Target age:** e.g. 3–5, 5–7, 7–9, 9–12.
- **Format:** picture book, read-aloud script, story video, short bedtime story, classroom story.
- **Length:** e.g. 300 words, 2 minutes, 8 scenes.
- **Core idea:** who wants what, what goes wrong?
- **Theme / lesson:** kindness, courage, honesty, sharing, patience, empathy, responsibility, respect for nature, trying again.
- **Tone:** warm, silly, adventurous, cozy, mysterious-but-safe.
- **Must include:** characters, setting, objects, repeated phrase, educational point.
- **Must avoid:** fear level, violence, sensitive themes, brands, real people, stereotypes.

Default assumptions if unspecified:

- Age: 4–8.
- Language: Traditional Chinese with Taiwan Mandarin vocabulary.
- Tone: warm, funny, lively, hopeful.
- Structure: 5–8 short scenes.
- Moral: one clear emotional lesson shown through action.
- Safety: no gore, no graphic violence, no horror, no humiliation, no dangerous imitation.

## Age Band Guidance

| Age | Story Fit | Language | Conflict | Length / Shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Naming, rhythm, warmth, caregiver bond | Very short, repetitive, sensory | Tiny surprise, comfort | 1–3 minutes, simple images |
| 3–5 | Concrete daily life, emotions, repetition | Short sentences, recurring phrases | Sharing, waiting, fear of dark, trying again | 3–6 scenes |
| 5–7 | Clear problem-solving, friendship, school/family | Lively spoken prose, some new words with context | Mild suspense, mistakes, teamwork | 5–8 scenes |
| 7–9 | More agency and cause-effect | Slightly richer dialogue and motivation | Responsibility, fairness, courage, jealousy | 8–12 scenes or short chapters |
| 9–12 | Nuanced motives, identity, belonging | More layered but still clear | Ethical tradeoff, self-doubt, community | Chapter-like structure |

For very young children, keep danger symbolic and brief. For older children, conflict can be deeper, but still avoid graphic harm or hopelessness.

## Core Story Principles

1. **One story, one heart.** Choose one central emotional lesson. Do not cram five morals into one story.
2. **Show, don't sermonize.** Let the character make choices, make mistakes, repair, and grow. Avoid ending with 「這個故事告訴我們……」 unless the user explicitly wants fable style.
3. **Child agency matters.** Children should solve or meaningfully help solve the problem. Adults may support, but should not magically fix everything.
4. **Warm conflict, safe landing.** A story needs tension, but children need reassurance, repair, or hope at the end.
5. **Concrete beats beat abstract lectures.** 「小熊把最後一塊餅乾分給妹妹」 is stronger than 「小熊學會分享的重要性」.
6. **Read-aloud first.** The text should sound good when spoken by a narrator.
7. **Fast start.** Introduce character and problem quickly. Avoid long background exposition.
8. **Humor without cruelty.** Use surprise, repetition, silly sounds, role reversal, and playful misunderstandings. Do not mock bodies, accents, poverty, disability, gender, or culture.
9. **台灣自然感.** Use everyday Taiwan settings and vocabulary naturally, not as token decoration.
10. **Leave room to breathe.** For story video scripts, write in short spoken chunks with scene-level pauses and sentence micro-pauses.

## Recommended Structure

Use this default arc for short children's stories:

1. **Warm opening:** introduce character, place, and mood.
2. **Wish or problem:** the character wants something or faces a small trouble.
3. **First try:** a simple attempt, often funny or imperfect.
4. **Second try:** stakes rise or misunderstanding grows.
5. **Choice point:** character notices what really matters.
6. **Repair / solution:** character acts with kindness, courage, honesty, or creativity.
7. **Gentle landing:** cozy ending, tiny joke, repeated phrase, or emotional closure.

For story videos, convert this into scenes:

```text
Scene 1: hook / warm setup
Scene 2: wish or problem appears
Scene 3: first attempt
Scene 4: funny complication
Scene 5: big choice
Scene 6: solution
Scene 7: warm ending / moral through image
```

## Traditional Chinese + Taiwan Usage

Default to **繁體中文** and Taiwan Mandarin. Prefer common Taiwan terms unless another locale is intentional.

Examples:

| Prefer Taiwan Usage | Avoid by Default |
|---|---|
| 腳踏車 | 自行车 |
| 捷運 | 地铁 |
| 便當 | 盒饭 |
| 幼兒園 | 幼儿园 / 幼稚园 if context differs |
| 國小 | 小学 |
| 垃圾車 | 垃圾车 |
| 阿公、阿嬤 | 爷爷、奶奶 if not intended |
| 公車 | 公交车 |
| 便利商店 | 便利店 if less Taiwan-flavored |
| 螢幕、影片 | 屏幕、视频 |
| 酪梨 | 牛油果 if Taiwan setting |

Taiwan-friendly settings:

- 夜市、便利商店、捷運站、公車站、騎樓、菜市場、圖書館、國小、幼兒園、公園、廟口、河濱、早餐店、里民活動中心。

Seasonal/cultural references:

- 春節、元宵節、端午節、中秋節、運動會、畢業典禮、梅雨季、颱風天、暑假、寒假。

Use these naturally. Do not force every story to be “about Taiwan.”

## Style Rules for Lively Children's Prose

Write like someone is telling the story to a child:

- Short sentences.
- Active verbs.
- Concrete images.
- Sensory details: sound, smell, touch, color, movement.
- Dialogue that sounds like people actually speak in Taiwan.
- Repeated phrases children can anticipate.
- Playful sound words: 「咚咚咚」、「呼——」、「啪答！」, used sparingly.
- Paragraphs short enough for narration and subtitles.
- Avoid dense explanation and adult metaphors.

Good pattern:

```text
小河馬阿噗最喜歡下雨天。
因為雨一來，屋簷就會唱歌：「滴答、滴答、滴滴答！」
可是今天，阿噗一點也笑不出來。
他的黃色雨鞋，不見了。
```

Avoid:

```text
阿噗在經歷一連串生命課題後，終於理解到合作精神對社會群體的重要性。
```

## Humor Toolkit

Safe humor choices:

- Repetition with variation: 「第一次……第二次……第三次竟然……」
- Silly sounds: 「噗嚕噗嚕」、「咻——啪！」
- Role reversal: tiny ant teaches big elephant.
- Gentle misunderstanding: character misreads a sign or takes a phrase literally.
- Object comedy: a hat keeps landing on the wrong head.
- Playful exaggeration: “他緊張到連尾巴都想請假。”
- Warm running gag that returns at the end.

Avoid:

- Humiliation as the joke.
- Body-shaming, poverty jokes, disability jokes, accent jokes.
- “Ugly = bad” or “pretty = good.”
- Violence as slapstick if it looks imitable or painful.
- Adult double meanings hidden in children's content.

## Moral / Meaning Design

Prefer **寓意藏在選擇裡**.

Good lessons for children:

- Kindness is active, not just nice words.
- Courage means trying even when nervous.
- Honesty helps repair trust.
- Sharing is easier when feelings are heard.
- Mistakes are chances to learn.
- Asking for help is wise.
- Friends can be different and still care.
- Nature and community deserve respect.

Technique:

1. Give the protagonist a want.
2. Put a small obstacle in the way.
3. Let the protagonist try a self-centered or mistaken solution.
4. Let them notice another person's feeling or a bigger truth.
5. Let them choose a better action.
6. Show the warmer result.

Avoid:

- Punishment-only morals.
- Shame as teaching.
- Adults lecturing for the whole ending.
- A lesson that contradicts child safety, e.g. “always obey strangers,” “never tell adults,” “be brave by doing dangerous things.”

## Child-Safety Rules

Hard avoid unless explicitly for an older-child educational context with careful framing:

- Gore, blood detail, torture, graphic injury.
- Sexual content or adult innuendo.
- Self-harm methods or suicidal content.
- Cruelty to animals.
- Realistic horror, jump scares, nightmare imagery.
- Weapons as cool aspirational props.
- Dangerous dares, pranks, challenges, or step-by-step risky behavior.
- Bullying shown as funny or deserved.
- Discrimination or stereotypes based on ethnicity, gender, disability, body type, accent, religion, class, nationality, family structure.
- Consumer manipulation: 「叫爸爸媽媽買給你」, scarcity pressure, parasocial guilt.

Allowed with care:

- Mild suspense.
- A not-too-scary villain.
- A small accident with no graphic detail.
- Sadness, frustration, jealousy, fear.
- A mistake that is repaired.

Safety framing:

- If danger appears, model safe choices: pause, ask for help, tell a trusted adult, stay with a group, wear protection, do not imitate.
- Resolve fear with reassurance, humor, or support.
- Let characters apologize, repair, and recover emotionally.

## Inclusion and Representation

Checklist:

- Characters have agency, personality, and interests beyond identity.
- Avoid gender-role defaults such as brave boys / caring girls unless intentionally subverted.
- Do not make villains identifiable by accent, disability, body type, poverty, nationality, or ethnicity.
- Use diverse families naturally: single parent, grandparents, siblings, adoptive families, etc., when relevant.
- Avoid using food, names, clothes, or accents as jokes.
- For Indigenous, Hakka, immigrant, disability, religion, or specific community representation, avoid tokenism and consider expert/sensitivity review.

## Story Video Script Add-ons

When the story will become an illustrated narration video:

- Write in scene-friendly chunks.
- Each paragraph should map to one visual beat.
- Prefer 6–10 scenes for a 1–3 minute MVP.
- Avoid too many characters or locations in one short story.
- Leave visual room: describe scenes with clear focal action.
- Mark pauses for narration:
  - sentence micro-pause: natural punctuation.
  - scene pre-roll: a short breath before the next paragraph begins.
  - emotional pause: after surprise, danger, realization, or moral turn.
- Keep subtitles in mind: 1–2 lines, phrase-level chunks, no dense paragraphs.

Example scene-ready paragraph:

```text
小兔米米抱著紅色小雨傘，站在騎樓下。
雨滴答滴答，像一群小鼓手在屋頂上練習。
她想去公園，可是水溝旁邊，傳來一聲小小的：「喵……」
```

## Rewrite / Polish Procedure

When polishing an existing draft:

1. Identify target age and format.
2. Summarize the current story's heart in one sentence.
3. Check child-safety issues.
4. Remove adult exposition and abstract moralizing.
5. Strengthen the protagonist's want and choice.
6. Add concrete Taiwan-friendly details only where natural.
7. Shorten sentences for read-aloud rhythm.
8. Add one running gag or repeated phrase if suitable.
9. Make the ending warm and emotionally complete.
10. Provide both:
    - a polished version
    - a short change log explaining what improved.

## Quality Rubric

Score each 1–5 before final delivery:

| Dimension | 5 Means |
|---|---|
| Age fit | Vocabulary, conflict, and length match the target child age. |
| Safety | No graphic, frightening, discriminatory, or imitable-risk content. |
| Story clarity | The want/problem/choice/resolution are easy to follow. |
| Emotional meaning | The moral emerges through action, not lecture. |
| Taiwan voice | Natural Traditional Chinese and Taiwan vocabulary. |
| Read-aloud rhythm | Sentences are short, musical, and breathable. |
| Humor/fun | Playful moments exist without cruelty. |
| Pacing | Starts quickly, escalates clearly, no long exposition. |
| Visualizability | Each beat can become a clear illustration or scene. |

If any score is under 4, revise before presenting as final.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Too preachy.** If the ending sounds like a teacher scolding, rewrite so the lesson appears through a choice and consequence.
2. **Too scary.** Children can handle tension, but not prolonged dread, gore, horror framing, or helplessness. Add reassurance and resolution.
3. **Too slow.** If nothing happens in the first 3–5 sentences, cut setup and start closer to the problem.
4. **Too adult.** Remove abstract terms, cynicism, sarcasm, and complicated motives for young audiences.
5. **Moral overload.** One story should not teach honesty, sharing, ecology, nutrition, and traffic safety all at once.
6. **Unsafe imitation.** Do not make risky behavior look fun, easy, or consequence-free.
7. **Mean humor.** If the joke requires someone to be humiliated, choose a different joke.
8. **Translationese / CN wording.** Check for simplified characters and Mainland-only phrases when the target is Taiwan Traditional Chinese.
9. **Stereotype shortcuts.** Do not encode villainy, foolishness, or goodness through body, accent, class, gender, ethnicity, disability, or appearance.
10. **Not video-ready.** If this is for story video, split into visual beats and leave breathing room for narration.

## Verification Checklist

Before finalizing a story:

- [ ] Target age is stated or reasonably assumed.
- [ ] Story is in Traditional Chinese with Taiwan usage.
- [ ] Main character has a clear want/problem.
- [ ] The child character has agency.
- [ ] One central moral/emotional lesson is visible through action.
- [ ] No gore, graphic violence, horror, adult innuendo, cruelty, or dangerous imitation.
- [ ] No stereotypes, body-shaming, accent jokes, or humiliation comedy.
- [ ] Sentences are short and read-aloud friendly.
- [ ] Pacing starts quickly and avoids long exposition.
- [ ] Ending gives reassurance, repair, warmth, humor, or wonder.
- [ ] If for video, paragraphs map to visual scenes and include pause-friendly rhythm.
